# EXECUTIVE STATEMENT

OneDraft™ is being developed as an AI-native architectural, engineering, and construction drafting and project-intelligence platform designed to transform the relationship between design intent, structured project information, geometry, technical documentation, standards, revisions, and the built environment.

The economic opportunity represented by OneDraft™ extends substantially beyond the conventional computer-aided design market.

At the narrowest level, OneDraft™ participates in the global markets for CAD, BIM, AEC software, engineering software, construction technology, project information management, digital engineering, and AI-enabled design.

At a broader level, OneDraft™ operates within the professional-services economy supporting architecture, engineering, construction, infrastructure, development, planning, and technical consulting.

At the largest level, OneDraft™ exists within the information economy supporting the global built environment, including the design, construction, modification, operation, maintenance, and documentation of buildings and infrastructure.

The resulting economic environment is measured not in billions alone, but in **multiple trillions of dollars annually**.

The distinction between these layers is essential.

The entire global construction economy is not OneDraft™'s software revenue opportunity. The engineering-services economy is not automatically OneDraft™'s serviceable market. Likewise, the CAD and BIM markets do not represent the entire economic value that OneDraft™ may influence.

Rather, these markets form a connected economic stack.

OneDraft™ seeks to establish itself as an intelligent software and information layer within that stack.

# 1. THE ONEDRAFT™ ECONOMIC THESIS

The conventional CAD paradigm primarily treats the drawing or model as the principal digital artifact.

OneDraft™ is designed around a different proposition.

**The project is the primary digital artifact.**

The drawing is a representation of that project.

A building begins with requirements.

Requirements become design intent.

Design intent becomes structured project information.

Structured information becomes spaces, elements, assemblies, parameters, constraints, relationships, and geometry.

Those relationships become views, drawings, sheets, schedules, annotations, dimensions, specifications, and technical documentation.

The documentation becomes part of construction.

Construction produces revisions, redlines, change records, as-built information, commissioning information, and eventually operational and asset information.

OneDraft™ is designed to maintain a computational relationship between these stages.

This distinction creates the fundamental economic opportunity.

If software only produces drawings, its economic value is largely tied to drawing production.

If software maintains the structured information underlying the drawings, it can participate in a much broader portion of the project lifecycle.

That is the strategic economic position of OneDraft™.
